## Goal

Provide the canonical file-level audit of every discovered
`test/**/*.test.ts` suite. Each row identifies the suite's behavioral owner,
observable contract, closest overlap, and the evidence work required before
any test or path change is admitted.

This reference remains `draft` while Phase 2 completes the case matrices and
settles the catalog. File size, runtime, and matcher shape are audit context,
not removal criteria.

## Inventory Contract

Each discovered test file appears exactly once and records:

- **Current path** — the repository-relative discovered test path.
- **Feature owner** — the product or stable platform family that owns the
  behavior.
- **Layer** — the direct/unit, adapter, action, command, Interactive,
  integration, or evidence/smoke boundary.
- **Tested boundary** — the specific seam exercised by the suite.
- **Representative contract** — a concrete trigger and observable outcome.
- **Closest overlap** — the nearest suite or suites requiring comparison.
- **Disposition** — the file-level next step defined below.
- **Rationale** — why the boundary is distinct or needs more evidence.
- **Case matrix** — `required in Phase 2` for `case audit` and
  `split review`; otherwise an em dash.

Phase 2 will add the literal case matrices. Until then, a required marker is a
gate, not permission to rename, merge, remove, split, or move a test. Any case
classified as `keep pending evidence` remains outside the removal scope.

## Disposition Meanings

- `retain` — one coherent contract family with no material overlap signal.
- `rename review` — useful ownership whose file or case names obscure the
  contract.
- `case audit` — possible vague, duplicate, or layered overlap requiring
  literal case comparison.
- `split review` — independently changing contract families share one suite.
- `move-only review` — useful coverage whose final catalog owner is unclear.
- `fixture/helper review` — support ownership may be duplicated, too broad,
  or misplaced.
- `keep pending evidence` — evidence is insufficient or disputed, so the
  affected case is retained unchanged and cannot be removed.
